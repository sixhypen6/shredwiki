1 Overview

Anchorfling Void, Hypersurge.

Void tech consists of two anchored blocks (the main build & the module) situated far apart and connected by a constraint (such as motor joints). It relies on a high-velocity fling module to force a separation between them.

Originally discovered as a method to bypass the workspace FallenPartsDestroyHeight limit (~-5000 units), Void Tech allows vehicles to operate below the kill barrier without instantly despawning.

Additionally, Void Tech automatically forces an Axis Lock state on the assembly. Beyond simply surviving the void, if the root part remains in motion, it heavily disrupts Roblox's server-client interpolation—resulting in positional surgetech, desynced invincibility, and extreme flinging mechanics when combined with secondary constraints.

2 Surge Effects

Surge is the primary observable side-effect of Void Tech. On the server side and for other players, the build appears to "go crazy" or violently jitter/fling across screen space. On the user's client side, movement remains smooth and controlled.

Depending on the strength, quality, and execution of the initial fling, several tiers of surge can occur:

  • Microsurge: Subtle interpolation jittering; harder for opponents to hit.
  • Hypersurge: Extreme visual desync covering massive areas of the map.
  • Invisibility: Client rendering falls out of bounds relative to server bounding boxes.

3 Charging Methods

NOTE: Anchorfling modules rely on physics solver timing and have a chance to fail on activation depending on PPD state assignment.

There are multiple ways to charge and activate a Void Tech module, with the most reliable methods relying on high-velocity void flings.

3.1 PPD-Based Charging

PPD (Physics Priority Distribution) charging relies on state assignment behaviors during anchor/unanchor sequences to force anchor constraints to separate cleanly and trigger bugged physics states.
