Antivoid Tech (commonly referred to as Root Displacement / RD Tech) is a high-tier physics mechanic in the Roblox engine. It allows a build to pass through all solid geometry (noclip) and gain virtual damage immunity across the main assembly.

Related Noclip Mechanics: Other specialized rootpart technologies also grant noclip characteristics, including Bancore (an advanced RD variant with constraint transfer properties) and Dimensional Tech.

1. Effects

The primary effects of Antivoid depend on the exact module configuration used. Different setups produce varying degrees of positional lock, clip ability, and rotational displacement.

1.1 Axis Lock

Axis lock becomes immediately noticeable upon activation when attempting to tilt or maneuver the build.

  • Module Variance: Non-anchorfling modules enforce strict directional axis locking, whereas standard Anchorfling variants do not provide axis lock.
  • Orientation Offset: Upon activation, the build may tilt unexpectedly or lock at an awkward angle (e.g., tilted 90°). Equipping a Gyro block is strongly recommended to maintain steady orientation.

1.2 Noclip

Noclip is the defining feature of Antivoid Tech, allowing full phase-through capabilities against terrain, boundaries, and enemy builds.

  • Damage Immunity: While active, the core build takes zero collision/physical damage. (Note: Ongoing testing is confirming whether individual module components take damage, though they likely share this immunity.)
  • Assembly Boundaries: Noclip is strictly tied to the parent physics assembly connected directly to the module. It does not automatically pass through un-merged mechanical joints.

1.3 Surge

Surge refers to rapid positional micro-displacements and directional updates.

  • Anchorfling vs. Standard Modules: Anchorfling modules produce no surge effects. Other Antivoid module types generate a spectrum of surge behavior, ranging from Microsurge to Normal Surge and high-frequency Hyper Surge.
  • Efficiency Note: For raw, high-activation surge output, Void Tech is generally preferred over Antivoid setups due to its higher activation rate.

2. Module Types

Antivoid mechanics are generated using specific structural and constraint setups:

  • Anchorfling: Uses Motor1 blocks (HingeConstraints) set to maximum torque/velocity to fling an anchor ~10,000 studs away from spawn. Provides stable noclip without axis locking or surge.
  • Anchor Charge: Storing and releasing constraint energy across locked joints to force root displacement on demand.

3. Assembly Isolation & Limitations

As detailed in Section 1.2 (Noclip), Antivoid is strictly localized to the primary assembly directly attached to the active module.

Assembly Behavior Rules:
  • No Passive Inheritance: Sub-assemblies (e.g., structures connected via an unlocked Motor2 or unlocked Motor1) do not inherit the noclip effect.
  • Creating Assemblies: Unlocking a motor joint creates a distinct physics assembly, immediately removing the noclip effect from the newly separated parts.
  • Re-merging Assemblies: Re-locking the motor joint merges the physics tree back into the main root assembly, transferring the noclip effect back to those parts.

4. Variants & Related Tech

  • Bancore (Advanced Root Displacement): An advanced variant of RD designed to bypass standard assembly isolation rules. Unlike base Antivoid, Bancore allows noclip properties to cross constraint boundaries.
  • Dimensional Tech: A specialized high-tier displacement method that provides noclip. (Documentation currently undergoing further testing).
