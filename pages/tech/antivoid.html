Antivoid Tech (also known as Root Displacement / RD Tech) grants a noclip effect and virtual damage immunity to affected structures without automatically transferring to separate assemblies.

1. Overview & Mechanics

All Antivoid methods involve flinging in some capacity (typically displacing an anchor around 10,000 studs).

  • Damage Resistance: Affected assemblies generally take no external damage.
  • Surge Variance: Generates variable surge ranging from minor 1-block position shifts to larger jitter, though typically milder than Void Tech surge.

2. Assembly Isolation & Motor Locking

Unlike standard physical states, Antivoid is strictly localized:

  • Detachment / Unlocking: When a new assembly is created (e.g., unlocking a motor joint), the AV state does not transfer to the new assembly.
  • Re-locking: Locking the motor back merges the physics tree, transferring the AV state back across the structure.

3. Anchorfling Requirements

A basic anchorfling setup relies on Motor1 connectors (utilizing HingeConstraints):

  • Motor1 Settings: Lock enabled, Powered, Toggle (optional), set to Maximum Speed and Torque.
  • Disconnect Trigger: A mechanism to fling part of a constraint so the anchor continuously gains/loses distance.
  • Anchor Block: Flung approximately 10k studs away from spawn.

4. Tech Types & Advanced Variants

  • Anchor Charge & Anchor Fling: Storing/releasing constraint momentum.
  • Remote Build (10k studs): Positioning core modules far from spawn to bypass spatial checks.
  • Bancore (Advanced RD): An advanced variant that does transfer across constraints while retaining noclip properties.
  • Dimensional Tech: A related high-tier displacement method known to grant noclip.
