1. Assembly Architecture & Hierarchy

An Assembly is a collection of parts connected via rigid joints (welds, motor locks, or active joints). The engine calculates physics for the entire assembly as a single rigid body.

1.1 AssemblyRootPart Selection Rules

Every assembly contains exactly one AssemblyRootPart. Roblox determines the root part dynamically using specific priority rules:

  • Mass Priority: Larger, denser, and heavier parts take priority for RootPart assignment.
  • Hierarchy Position: Direct children of the primary assembly root take higher assignment priority than distant branches.
  • Center of Physics: Thruster forces, gyro torques, and network position updates are calculated directly relative to the designated AssemblyRootPart.

1.2 Destruction & RootPart Reassignment

When cutter blocks destroy the block designated as the AssemblyRootPart, the engine instantly evaluates the remaining welded geometry and assigns a new root part. This sudden shift alters the vehicle's center of mass instantly, frequently causing control desync or rapid rotational instability.

2. Engine Gravity & Physics Hibernation

2.1 Massless vs. Gravity Calculations

Toggling Massless = true on an assembly strips its mass variable, but does not grant immunity to gravity.

  • Gravity applies constant acceleration ($9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$) across all unanchored parts regardless of mass values.
  • Massless assemblies fall at identical rates to heavy assemblies unless countered by upward thrust or aerodynamic lift.

2.2 Physics Sleep State (Hibernation)

To conserve server resources, Roblox's solver puts stationary or low-velocity unanchored assemblies into a Physics Sleep state.

  • Sleeping assemblies pause physical force, torque, and constraint updates until actived by external collision.
  • Prevention: Integrating a constantly active Motor2 keeps the rotational velocity magnitude above 1.0, forcing the solver thread to keep the assembly permanently awake.
