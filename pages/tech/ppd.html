Physics Priority Distribution (PPD) is an engine-level physics state mechanic. Most of the research on PPD was pioneered by KAMEON. It explains why certain anchor toggles trigger extreme physics anomalies while others behave normally.

1. Mechanics & Anchor States

Whenever a part or assembly is unanchored or re-anchored, the engine assigns an internal solver priority value to it.

Because this assignment happens dynamically upon toggling anchors, anchorfling and separation modules have a chance-based activation rate depending on which priority value the engine assigns to the module during its unanchor sequence.

2. Binary State Assigning (0 vs 1)

In simplified terms, the state assignment operates like a binary value (0 or 1):

  • Normal State: Standard solver behavior where physics updates and positional constraints behave deterministically on both server and client.
  • Bugged Physics State: An anomalous priority state where constraint calculations produce erratic velocities, desynced server-client interpolation, or forced separation across anchor blocks.
CREDIT: Research on anchor priority assignment and state distribution credit goes to KAMEON.

3. Relation to Void Tech

PPD is the underlying engine cause for why Void Tech modules sometimes fail or succeed upon spawning or charging. Getting assigned the bugged priority state during an anchorfling is what causes the build to separate across constraints and enter a surge state.
