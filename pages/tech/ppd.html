Physics Priority Distribution (PPD) is a low-level engine mechanic within Roblox that dictates how physics calculations and solver priorities are assigned to assemblies when state changes occur. Most of the foundational research on PPD was conducted by KAMEON.

1. Overview & Mechanics

When a part toggles its anchored state—specifically during unanchoring or re-anchoring sequences—the physics engine assigns a internal priority state value to the affected assembly.

Depending on this assigned value, the game engine processes the assembly's velocity, constraint updates, and server-client network synchronization differently.

2. Binary Priority States

The assigned state effectively operates as a binary flag (conceptually represented as 0 or 1):

  • Standard Physics (Value A): The assembly behaves strictly under standard Roblox physics solver constraints. Interpolation between client and server remains synchronized.
  • Bugged / Desynced State (Value B): The assembly is handled with altered priority in the physics pipeline. Under this state, constraints can yield unintended torque, delayed positional updates, or extreme velocity multiplication when coupled with anchor-flings.
RESEARCH NOTE: Credit to KAMEON for isolating PPD state behavior during anchor-reanchor physics testing.

3. Applications in Void Tech

PPD manipulation is the driving factor behind inconsistent or high-potency Void Tech charges. Because anchorfling modules rely on forced separation between assembly roots, landing a "bugged" PPD state upon unanchoring is often what causes hyper-surging or instantaneous void clipping.
