Physics Priority Distribution (PPD) is an engine-level physics state mechanic. Most foundational research on PPD was pioneered by KAMEON. It explains why certain anchor toggles trigger extreme physics anomalies and separation surges while others behave normally.

1. Mechanics & Anchor States

Whenever a part or assembly is unanchored or re-anchored, the Roblox physics engine assigns an internal solver priority value to it.

Because this assignment happens dynamically upon toggling anchors, anchorfling and separation modules exhibit a chance-based activation rate. Success depends entirely on which priority value the engine allocates to the module during its unanchor sequence.

2. Binary State Assignment (0 vs 1)

In simplified technical terms, the priority assignment operates like a binary state value (0 or 1):

  • Normal State (0): Standard solver behavior where physics updates and positional constraints behave deterministically across both server and client.
  • Bugged Physics State (1): An anomalous priority state where constraint calculations produce erratic velocities, desynced server-client interpolation, or forced physical separation across anchor blocks.
CREDIT: Research on anchor priority assignment and state distribution is credited to KAMEON.

3. Relation to Void Tech & Surge States

PPD is the primary engine-level cause behind why Void Tech modules succeed or fail upon spawning or charging.

Receiving the bugged priority state during an anchorfling sequence forces the build assembly to separate across constraint joints, initiating a high-velocity physics surge.
