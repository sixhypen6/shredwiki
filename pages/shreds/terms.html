Shredder Terms & Terminology

A standardized reference index clearing up shredder community slang, historical naming shifts, structural framework distinctions, and combat exploit mechanics.

1. Mechanical Distinctions & Terminology Shifts

1.1 Cutter Enhancement (CE) vs. Demean

Much of the confusion around these terms comes from a historical shift in community slang:

  • Historical Context: "Demean" was originally used by the community to describe target motor speed numbers (e.g., 47.124, 31.416). As building methods evolved, the term Cutter Enhancement (CE) was adopted to describe motor speed tuning, while Demean shifted to mean blade clipping and recoil dampening.
  • Cutter Enhancement (CE): Refers to setting your motor speeds so your blades spin in perfect sync with the game's physics ticks, ensuring maximum damage per hit.
  • Demean Mechanics: Refers strictly to stopping recoil and absorb shocks. This allows blades to pass through objects (blade clipping) without your shredder core getting flung away when you crash into another build.
  • Damp Blade: An extra setup attached to your blades specifically to soak up structural shock when hitting an enemy craft.

1.2 Full Massless (Desync) vs. True Anti-Gravity

A shredder floating motionlessly in mid-air is often mislabeled as "Anti-Grav," but different mechanics cause it:

  • Anti-Gravity / Weightless Setup: Normal builds counter gravity using Hover Thrusters set to Max Drag and 0 Speed. Max drag locks your craft in 3D space, letting heavy shredders float cleanly and turn instantly without falling.
  • Full Massless (Desync Fake): Happens when every single block on a build has its weight set to zero. The game engine breaks trying to figure out where the build is, causing severe lag for other players. On their screens, your build looks like it's frozen floating in space, but it's just a server visual bug rather than real anti-gravity stability.

1.3 Boundary Tech & Position Tricks (Surge & NaN)

Advanced build setups can trick the game into hiding your core or freezing enemy players:

  • Surge Mechanics: A server trick where your build rapidly teleports or flickers between several spots on the map. This tricks target locks and enemy missiles into aiming at an empty spot, while you keep moving normally on your screen.
  • NaN Tech (+NaN / -NaN): An aggressive glitch exploit using motor setups that completely breaks any enemy build on contact. Getting hit by NaN is unrecoverable—your craft stops working and your camera freezes permanently at the exact angle you were aiming when you got hit. Comes in both +NaN and -NaN versions.

2. Structural Frameworks: Skeleton vs. Exoskeleton

How you wire up your blocks determines if your shredder stays together or gets torn apart in battle.

2.1 Skeleton (Base Core)

The Skeleton is the main inner spine or core frame of your shredder. It's the central block chain where your primary parts—like motors, gyros, and thrusters—are mounted.

2.2 Exoskeleton (Invincible Weld Frame)

An Exoskeleton is a protective outer weld frame that keeps blocks connected even if the middle parts get destroyed.

  • Without an Exoskeleton (Standard Chain): If Block A connects to the core, Block B connects to A, and Block C connects to B—destroying Block A will break off both B and C, causing them to fall to the ground.
  • With an Exoskeleton (Invincible Weld Frame): Blocks A, B, and C are all individually welded to an invincible outer harness block. If an enemy cutter completely destroys Block A or B, Block C stays attached to the frame, floating and fully working.
